Five color mistakes a thoughtful stylist avoids

Beautiful color is not only about the formula. It begins with honest consultation, respect for the hair, and a plan the client can actually maintain.

Hair color should be designed around the person wearing it. A reference photo can start the conversation, but it cannot tell a stylist everything about your starting level, previous color, condition, texture, or day-to-day routine.

1. Rushing the consultation

A strong consultation covers color history, chemical services, at-home products, heat habits, desired upkeep, and what the client means by words such as bright, warm, or natural. Good questions prevent mismatched expectations.

2. Treating the reference photo as a formula

The same shade behaves differently on different starting canvases. A professional interprets the inspiration, then adapts placement, tone, and timing for the hair in the chair.

3. Ignoring condition and elasticity

The healthiest choice may be a staged transformation, a softer result, or treatment before more lightening. Protecting the integrity of the hair is part of the design.

4. Skipping tests when the history is unclear

A strand or compatibility test can reveal how a small section responds before a larger commitment is made. It is especially useful when previous color or chemical history is uncertain.

5. Finishing without an upkeep plan

Before you leave, you should understand recommended home care, heat protection, refresh timing, and what fading may look like. The best color plan includes the weeks after the appointment.

Bring the whole story

For a color consultation, bring inspiration photos and be candid about previous services and home products. That information helps Teddy recommend a result that looks beautiful now and makes sense later.
